What this feature is about

- The Study plan keeps its wide main column: headline, scope, offer, Rebuild,
  Start and the two facts.
- A right-hand tabbed panel holds the secondary material: the tutor and hints,
  what to expect, and why these cards.
- Previous sessions become a plain list in the main column, replacing the place
  the three disclosures used to take: the last five ended sessions, newest
  first.
- It does NOT add a history page, search, filters, per-card statistics, or a new
  store.

Questions and answers locked so far

1. "the study is scanty. So keep the wide."
Answer:
- The plan keeps a single wide main column. It is not squeezed into a narrow
  rail beside a second column of content.
- The secondary material moves out of that column, so the main column keeps its
  width for the plan itself.

Why:
- The learner asked for width, not for more panels under the plan.
- Source: ticket study-sidebar-and-history.md, Resolution; learner voice note
  2026-10-26.

2. Why move "why these cards" and "what to expect" to a right-hand tabbed panel?
Answer:
- On the plan screen only, a right-hand panel of about 320px holds three tabs:
  "Tutorial and hints" (the existing tutor disclosure, unchanged in content),
  "What to expect", and "Why these cards".
- The panel is hidden while a card is running, matching the locked rule that
  plan material disappears during review.
- Added 2026-10-27 at the learner's request: the panel caps at the viewport
  height and scrolls its own body, so a long "Why these cards" list never
  stretches the plan page. The tab row stays in place.

Why:
- The reasons and expectations belong beside the offer, at the same glance,
  instead of three stacked disclosures under it.
- Source: ticket, Resolution; learner typed 2026-10-27: "main area = previous
  sessions, right sidebar = the moved panels".

3. "previous sessions to take the old place of those dropdowns in the main
   area, as a short list rather than a dropdown."
Answer:
- A plain list, never a disclosure and never a dropdown, in the main column where
  the three disclosures used to sit (under the Start row).
- It shows the last five ended sessions, newest first: the local date and time,
  the scope it drew from, "answered / offered", and whether it finished or was
  stopped.
- Each row whose scope is known has one "Learn again" action. It rebuilds a
  fresh plan on that session's pack and deck; the learner still presses Start.
  The exact cards are not replayed.
- The hard cap is five. There is no "show more" in this pass.

Why:
- Five gives continuity without turning the plan into a long wall, the same
  reason the learner asked for five and no more.
- Source: ticket, Resolution and agent decisions.

4. What if there is no history?
Answer:
- One calm line says no finished sessions yet. The list is not hidden and no
  empty box is drawn.

Why:
- An empty area looks broken; one sentence explains the state.
- Source: ticket, Resolution; [agent decision] wording.

5. What happens on a phone?
Answer:
- Below 860px the right panel folds into one disclosure under the plan, titled
  "Plan details". Opening it shows the three sections stacked; the plan column
  loses no width.
- The previous sessions list stays in the main column and simply narrows.

Why:
- The phone must not lose the plan width, and three tabs inside a tiny aside
  would be cramped.
- Source: ticket agent decisions; [agent decision] title and stacked sections.

6. Where does the "scope it drew from" come from? The session record does not
   carry it today.
Answer:
- Add optional `pack` and `deck` fields to the session's started fact, written
  when the session starts. They are the same values the scope picker uses: the
  pack name and the deck id. Both empty means the whole library.
- Older sessions have no fields; their row leaves the scope out and has no
  "Learn again" action.
- No new store is made.

Why:
- The session fact already holds offered, answered, completed and stopped; the
  scope is the one missing fact the learner asked to see.
- A display string would not be enough to start a session again, so the fields
  keep the values the plan endpoint needs.
- Guessing the scope from today's library would be wrong after a rename or a
  delete.
- Source: `v1/src/lib/shared/types.ts:156-164`,
  `v1/src/lib/browser/router.ts:307-312`; [agent decision].

7. Which tab is open first?
Answer:
- "Tutorial and hints" (or "Hints" when no tutor is set up). It is the tab that
  can ask the learner to make a choice, so it comes first, as in the ticket.
- No tab choice is stored; the plan screen opens on the first tab each time.

Why:
- The tutor choice should not be buried behind a tab the learner may never open.
- Source: ticket order; [agent decision].

8. What must not change?
Answer:
- The Progress screen, the algorithm, the session loop, the Resume snapshot, the
  scope picker and the offer control stay as they are.
- The three moved disclosures keep their existing words and controls; only their
  container changes.
- No new store. The only new data is the two optional fields on a started
  session (Q6), written to the existing session facts.

Why:
- This is a layout and a light history view, not a new session model.
- Source: ticket "Deliberately not done"; [agent decision].

9. "the previous lessons should have details and can be re learnt"
Answer:
- Details stay plain: the row itself is the detail (time, scope, answered /
  offered, finished or stopped). No expander, no card list, no per-card answers.
- "Learn again" rebuilds a fresh plan on the same pack and deck. It does not
  replay the exact cards, and it does not skip the plan: the learner still
  presses Start.

Why:
- A card-level replay would need the session's card list saved, which the
  learner did not ask for now; it would turn a light list into history storage.
- Keeping the plan as the offer preserves the tutor choice and the Start gate.
- Source: learner message 2026-10-27, verbatim above; [agent decision] for the
  fresh-plan wording.
