Q1. Is this a new pack, a course, or a replacement for the Dictionary work?
A. A new built-in pack. The Course Dictionary work on its own branch is not
   touched. The learner asked for system architecture and system design
   combined into one library.
   Why: the learner named a missing subject and said "create a pack".
   Rules out: editing the Dictionary branch, or shipping an external pack the
   learner must install.

Q2. Built-in means what, exactly?
A. The pack ships inside the app: its folder is read by the seed builder, its
   bundle is written to public/packs, and a fresh install receives it with no
   import step. The learner confirmed "Verify it should be an inbuilt course."
   Rules out: a downloadable pack, a demo, or a folder that exists but is not
   in the seed list.

Q3. How wide is the course?
A. Ten decks, ten cards each, one hundred cards. The decks follow a learner's
   path: foundations, requirements, styles, data, reliability, security,
   designing AI systems, AI-assisted engineering, delivery, CI/CD.
   Why: the built-in pack tests fix ten cards per deck; a course-shaped library
   needs a visible start, middle and end. Ten decks keeps every deck a full
   session and every subject a real deck.
   Rules out: a loose pile of cards with no sequence.

Q4. What does "for the AI era" change?
A. Two whole decks: designing AI systems (LLMs, RAG, evals, grounding, token
   budgets, prompt injection) and AI-assisted engineering (review gates,
   generated-code security, tests, specs, provenance, accountability). The rest
   of the course still teaches the durable ideas, because AI does not remove
   boundaries, data ownership, reliability or delivery.
   Rules out: a course that is only about prompting.

Q5. What counts as evidence and documentation?
A. Every deck names the book, paper, standard or vendor documentation behind
   it in SOURCES.md. Every photo has a creator and licence. Every video is a
   real clip checked through YouTube oEmbed. Every diagram is original and
   stored as an SVG in the pack.
   Why: the learner said "gather all the evidence, all the documentation."
   Rules out: unattributed images, dead video ids, and cards invented from
   memory without a source.

Q6. Video or animation?
A. A verified short video where one exists and fits. Where no video exists, or
   a clip would not show the idea clearly, an original animated SVG. The app
   supports built-in images, not built-in video files, so an animation ships as
   an image and plays inside the teaching layer.
   Why: the learner asked for animation "where you cannot find videos".
   Rules out: downloading YouTube files, shipping large video binaries, or
   leaving a card with no visual teaching.

Q7. How much media per deck?
A. At least one photograph or static diagram, at least one animation or
   comparison diagram, and at least one video. Every card carries a teaching
   layer. The pack ships 10 photographs, 10 animated SVGs, 8 static SVGs and 24
   verified video clips.
   Rules out: a deck that is only text, or a course where media is concentrated
   in the first two decks.

Q8. Does this change the app's schema?
A. No. The pack uses the existing card and teaching schema, the existing seed
   builder and the existing static export. Only the seed list and the seed
   tests change.
   Why: the pack format is the extension point; a new subject should not need
   new code.
   Rules out: a special "course" screen, a new database table, or a runtime
   server.

Q9. What must never happen?
A. No commit, push, pull request or merge without the learner's explicit go.
   The Course Dictionary branch stays untouched. The pack must not break the
   two existing built-in packs, their decks or their images.
   Rules out: mixing this work into the Dictionary branch, or "finishing" by
   pushing unasked.
