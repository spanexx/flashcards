GRILL: Word lessons
Status: Locked
Date: 2026-11-21
Pack: word-lessons
Authority: the learner supplied a full feature spec on 2026-11-21 and answered
three blocking questions the same day. Every other rule below is an agent
decision that follows from that spec and from the existing locked packs
(teaching-layers, course-dictionary, creation-control, language-prompt-control).

Source spec, quoted from the learner:
- "We are upgrading our flashcard system specifically for language learning"
- "We are not copying the Loopworks game mechanics (no drone, board, or puzzle
  gates). Instead, we are adopting its pedagogical structure: teaching one focus
  word in depth - showing how it inflects/changes across contexts, why it
  changes, and testing it through progressive stages including matching."
- "This word layer must remain optional at the pack/card level so non-language
  decks (e.g., System Design, Browser Dev) continue working unchanged."
- "Do not reinvent dictionary lookups or pedagogical explanations. Integrate
  directly with our existing dictionary and teaching layer"
- "Language packs can define structured Word entries containing: base word /
  lemma and core meaning; inflected/changed forms; explicit explanation of what
  changed and why; matching pairs"
- "Extend the Card model with optional fields linking a card to a wordId and a
  stage (story / cloze, spelling / type, forms, dialogue / picks, matching)"
- "Forms Stage: Explicitly presents the transformation (base -> changed form),
  explains why the change happens (using the teaching layer), and has the
  learner apply the changed form in a fresh sentence. (Skip automatically if a
  word has no inflections.)"
- "Matching Mode: A dedicated flashcard interaction allowing learners to pair
  target-language words/forms with their exact meanings."
- "Add an optional focusWord field to the pack/lesson creation brief (and
  pre-fill or validate against the dictionary where available)."
- "Update both the provider generation prompt and the copy-paste chat prompt so
  the LLM builds a cohesive single-word lesson rather than disconnected lines."
- "Validate incoming word lessons strictly ... reject it cleanly with a plain,
  human-readable error message instead of rendering a broken or empty card."

Q1. Where does a Word record live?
A1. Inside the pack bundle, as a `words` array beside `cards`. A word is
    identified by `id`, scoped to its pack. Reasons: import is all-or-nothing,
    so a lesson travels as one file; the loader already reads one bundle per
    pack; a second file would need a second loader, a second error path and a
    partial-state rule. A cards fragment may add cards that link to a word that
    already exists in the target pack, but may not define words itself.
    [learner answer, 2026-11-21]

Q2. Do `wordId` and `stage` change a card's identity hash?
A2. No. `canonicalContent` stays exactly as it is, so adding a word link to a
    shipped card never resets that card's evidence. `wordId` and `stage` are
    context, not content: they never change what the learner is asked or judged
    on. The collision risk is nil in practice because a story card and a forms
    card carry different prompt and answer text. The rule is written into the
    WHY comment on `canonicalContent` so a later change cannot undo it silently.
    [learner answer, 2026-11-21]

Q3. Does a Matching card record one answer or one answer per pair?
A3. One answer for the whole card. Every pair must be right for `got_it`; a
    partly finished or partly wrong board is `nearly`; nothing matched before
    the learner gives up is `no`. This keeps the evidence model unchanged, needs
    no new table, and means progress per word is derived from the same rows as
    every other card.
    [learner answer, 2026-11-21]

Q4. Is the word layer required for a pack?
A4. No. `words` is optional and defaults to `[]`; `wordId` and `stage` are
    optional on every card. A pack with no words behaves exactly as it does
    today. A card with no `wordId` renders exactly as it does today. The
    System Design and Browser Dev packs are untouched and are the regression
    proof.
    [spec: "must remain optional at the pack/card level"]

Q5. What exactly is in a Word?
A5. `id` (stable, lowercase, unique in the pack), `lemma` (the base form),
    `meaning` (one plain line in the pack's source language), optional `lang`,
    `forms` (each with `label`, `to`, `meaning`, `why`, optional `use`), `pairs`
    (each with `target` and `meaning`), and optional `note`. Every meaning is in
    the pack's source language, never hardcoded English: the learner may be a
    Polish speaker learning English, and the model must serve that pair too.
    `why` is a `TeachingPart[]`, not a string, so the existing teaching layer's
    text, image and video parts work unchanged and the app does not grow a
    second explanation format.
    [agent decision, following the spec's four required pieces]
    [learner clarification 2026-11-21: "by english word i meant the language user
    is learning from not hard coded"]

Q6. What is a `stage`, and what does each stage permit?
A6. `stage` is one of `story`, `spelling`, `forms`, `dialogue`, `matching`. The
    stage fixes the answer mode, so a mislabelled card is refused instead of
    rendering something surprising:
      story    -> cloze
      spelling -> type
      forms    -> type
      dialogue -> picks
      matching -> match
    `wordId` and `stage` travel together: a card may have both or neither.
    A stage without a word is a broken link and is refused.
    [agent decision, from the spec's stage-to-mode list]

Q7. Is `matching` a new answer mode?
A7. Yes. `AnswerMode` gains `match`. The pairs are not copied onto the card:
    they come from the linked Word at render time, so there is one source of
    truth and a pack cannot drift. A `match` card without a `wordId` is refused.
    [agent decision; the alternative, duplicating pairs per card, repeats the
    same data five times and drifts]

Q8. Is Forms a new answer mode?
A8. No. A Forms card is an ordinary `type` card whose prompt shows
    `base -> blank` and whose help is the form's `why`. The learner types the
    changed form in the card's own sentence. The form's `use` text stays in the
    word record as authoring material and is not rendered as a second blank:
    printing it would show the changed form before the learner has tried. This
    reuses the existing judging, evidence and near-miss rules, and it keeps the
    only new interaction surface in the app to Matching.
    [agent decision, from the spec's "apply the changed form in a fresh
    sentence"; narrowed by the no-leak rule]
    NOTE 2026-11-22 (D-49): new authoring prompts should write the request in
    words (`Type the changed form of "..." :`), not with a literal `___` text
    part. A real blank part is for cloze cards only; a type-card judge ignores
    blank inputs. Shipped cards are not rewritten.

Q9. How are the dictionary and the teaching layer used?
A9. In one fixed order, in one resolver:
      1. the card's own `teaching`, when the pack wrote it;
      2. the Word's `note` and the relevant form's `why`;
      3. the card's Dictionary page parts.
    The first non-empty source wins for the pre-answer help, and the page stays
    reachable as the deep material. No new `PageLevel` is added. The Dictionary
    page id already names the card, and a word's material belongs in the Word
    record because it must validate, travel in the bundle and reach the prompt.
    Adding a `word` page level would touch the page store, router, backup, seed,
    export and the Dictionary UI for no gain in this cut.
    [agent decision; the spec asks for integration, not a new page level]

Q10. How does the learner actually walk the five stages?
A10. Cards of the same word stay together and run in stage order. The plan
     orders them: `story, spelling, forms, dialogue, matching`, and a word's
     cards are adjacent when more than one is selected. A stage the pack did not
     write is simply absent. Forms is skipped automatically when the word has no
     forms. The runner never invents a card that is not in the library, so the
     offer count and the plan's promise stay true.
     [agent decision, from the spec's "intentional sequence"]

Q11. What does the learner see above a word card?
A11. One compact word line: the lemma and its meaning before the answer, with
     the changed form and its label added only once the answer is on screen, so
     the line never leaks the answer. The form's `why` is shown as help at the
     level's chosen moment. It appears only when the card has a `wordId`, so a
     plain card is unchanged. On the Matching card the same line names the word;
     the board below carries the pairs.
     [agent decision]

Q12. What does `focusWord` do in the creation brief?
A12. It is one optional text field on the brief, trimmed like every other text
     field. It reaches both prompt lanes as a hard instruction: build one
     lesson around this word. It also reaches the pack's `words` entry as the
     expected `lemma`. Validation is against the learner's installed packs only,
     because the app has no global lexicon: if an installed pack already has a
     word with that lemma, the Create screen offers it as a suggestion and shows
     its language. It never blocks a word the library has not seen.
     [agent decision; the spec says "validate against the dictionary where
     available", and the only dictionary the app owns is the learner's pages]

Q13. What does a word-lesson prompt ask for?
A13. Both lanes ask for one cohesive lesson, not five unrelated lines: the
     lemma, its meaning, its forms with a plain reason for each change, the
     matching pairs, and exactly the stage cards the word supports. The JSON
     shape includes `words` beside `cards`, and every generated card names its
     `wordId` and `stage`. The prompt states the stage-to-mode rule so the
     provider cannot produce a `story` card with `picks`.
     [spec: "cohesive single-word lesson rather than disconnected lines"]

Q14. What makes an imported word lesson invalid?
A14. Refused, all-or-nothing, with a plain sentence naming the card and the
     field: a duplicate or malformed word id; a card naming a word the pack does
     not define; `wordId` without `stage` or `stage` without `wordId`; a stage
     whose answer mode is wrong; a `forms` card on a word with no forms; a
     `matching` card on a word with fewer than two distinct pairs; a form with a
     missing `to`, `meaning` or `why`; a pair with an empty side; two pairs with the
     same target or the same meaning. The message reads like
     `Card 3 ("Piję kawę"): stage "forms" needs at least one form on word
     "kawa".`
     [spec: "reject it cleanly with a plain, human-readable error message"]

Q15. What is explicitly out of scope?
A15. No drone, board, puzzle gate or level-solve mechanic. No global dictionary
     service. No new page level. No per-pair evidence. No word-level progress
     table; word progress is read from its cards' rows. No change to the
     identity hash. No change to any non-language pack.
     [spec: "We are not copying the Loopworks game mechanics"]
