Q1. What does "plain and simple" mean for the card?
A. A monochrome card. The border and shadow are neutral, the outcome no longer
   washes the card or colours its border, the answer rule, hint block, teaching
   layer bar and word line are neutral, and the card's primary action is ink on
   paper in the light theme and paper on ink in the dark theme. The serif prompt
   and the existing layout stay. Outcome words still name the result; colour is
   no longer the signal.
   Why: the learner said "too much color" and "plain and simple". Removing the
   green tint from the card surface is the smallest change that answers it and
   leaves the rest of the app's palette alone.
   Rules out: a new theme, a new palette, a full app restyle, and removing the
   serif reading face.
   Source: learner message; `v1/src/app/globals.css` study card block.

Q2. Does the automatic mode line stay under the prompt?
A. Yes. "Fill the blank in the Polish line." is a neutral mode hint, not the
   pack's meta-prompt. It stays. Only the pack's own studyInstruction leaves the
   card.
   Why: the learner named `pack.studyInstruction` as the problem, and the mode
   line still helps a beginner know what to do.
   Rules out: deleting all instruction copy from the card.
   Source: learner message; `v1/src/lib/client/study-view.ts:22-53`.

Q3. Where does the study goal live?
A. The Library Packs panel shows a "Study goal" line in the pack row whenever
   the pack has a studyInstruction. It sits with the version, deck count, card
   count and language pair. The pack editor keeps its editable field. No new
   drawer is added to the session head.
   Why: the learner offered "Pack Overview / Deck Info view" as the destination,
   and the Packs panel is the app's pack overview. A line there is reachable
   without adding a second navigation surface.
   Rules out: printing the note on every card, a new session-header drawer, and
   hiding the note entirely.
   Source: learner message; `v1/src/app/library/PacksPanel.tsx:202-209`.

Q4. What happens to packs that already store a raw meta-prompt?
A. The card stops printing it at once. No data is rewritten. The pack editor
   still shows the stored text so the learner can replace it. New generation
   stores a distilled note; an old pack keeps its old text until the learner
   edits it.
   Why: silently rewriting learner data is not allowed, and the learner can
   already edit the field.
   Rules out: a migration that rewrites packs, and deleting the field.
   Source: learner message; `v1/src/app/library/PackEditor.tsx:148-157`.

Q5. How is the authoring skill versioned?
A. `AUTHORING_SKILL_VERSION` goes from 6.0.0 to 7.0.0, because the prompt text
   and the rules it teaches change. The provider generation prompt gets the same
   rules but has no version constant.
   Why: the version is the copy target's contract; a changed contract needs a
   new version.
   Rules out: keeping 6.0.0 and pretending the prompt did not change.
   Source: `v1/src/lib/browser/authoring.ts:22-23`.
   NOTE 2026-11-22 (D-49): the worked examples were tightened again, so the
   current version is 8.0.0. The 6.0.0 -> 7.0.0 line above is the history of
   this pack's first delivery, not a still-current value.

Q6. What does the authoring prompt say about self-contained cloze prompts?
A. Every cloze or transformation prompt must name the base word, owner, or
   context in the prompt when the sentence alone cannot tell the learner what to
   supply. The prompt shows both examples: "Base word: cup. There are two ___ on
   the table." and "Owner: Anna. That is ___ winter coat." The final self-check
   repeats the rule.
   Why: the learner named both failures and asked for a prompt instruction.
   Rules out: relying on the deck name, the previous card, or a picture to carry
   the missing subject.
   Source: learner message.

Q7. What is the code validation rule?
A. A pure helper refuses two mechanical cases in `hardRuleIssues`:
   1. A possessive expected answer ("Anna's") needs its owner ("Anna") as a
      whole word in the prompt.
   2. A prompt with a count word ("two", "three", "a pair", "several") and a
      regular plural expected answer ("cups") needs the singular base ("cup")
      as a whole word in the prompt.
   The message tells the author what to write, e.g. "write Base word: cup.
   before the sentence." The rule is narrow on purpose: the prompt instruction
   covers the judgment cases the app cannot know, such as an irregular plural.
   Why: the learner asked for a validation rule. These two cases are checkable
   without a dictionary or a grammar engine, and they are the two examples the
   learner gave.
   Rules out: a heuristic that guesses at every missing subject and refuses
   good cards such as "The capital of France is ___."
   Source: learner message; `v1/src/lib/core/card.ts:124-177`.

Q8. How is the study note distilled?
A. The prompts tell the generator to write one short learner-facing sentence in
   the question language that says how to study. It must not address the AI, say
   "your job", mention cards, decks, JSON or generation, or copy the learner's
   request. The example shape carries a placeholder, never the learner's raw
   words. The generated note wins over the raw brief, and the app no longer
   writes the raw brief onto the pack.
   Why: the learner named the raw meta-prompt as the leak. Preferring the
   generator's distilled note is the only way the new instruction has an effect.
   Rules out: storing the brief verbatim, and keeping the old "brief wins"
   precedence.
   Source: learner message; `v1/src/lib/provider/generate.ts:327-333`,
   `v1/src/lib/shared/creation.ts:196-204`.

Q9. Does the provider generation lane get the same rules?
A. Yes. The pack, deck and cards generation prompts get the self-contained
   prompt rule and the study-note rule. The app's own default template also
   states them, so a pack with no custom template is covered.
   Why: the learner's example cards came from generation, and the app has two
   generation lanes (provider and chat). One rulebook cannot disagree with
   itself.
   Rules out: fixing only the copyable chat prompt while provider generation
   keeps the old habits.
   Source: `v1/src/lib/provider/prompt.ts:44-59`, `:218-254`.

Q10. What does not change?
A. The judge, outcomes, evidence rows, scheduling, session loop, one-return
   rule, hint cap, provider request shape, storage shape, import/export/backup
   fields, and the Dictionary pages do not change. The automatic mode line stays.
   The pack editor still owns the study instruction field. No new dependency is
   added.
   Why: this is a card-surface and prompt-language change, not a new learning
   model.
   Rules out: a new evidence field, a new route, a new provider call, and a new
   settings screen.
   Source: the existing locked packs and the learner's scope.
