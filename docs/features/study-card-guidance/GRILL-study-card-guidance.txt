Q1. What counts as an attempt?
A. A typed card needs at least one non-space character. A cloze card needs at
   least one filled blank; the other blanks may stay empty and are judged as
   misses. A picks card keeps its existing rule: one choice selected.
   Why: the learner said "we dont allow no input ... must attempt to answer".
   Spaces are not input; a partly filled cloze is a real try.
   Rules out: an empty submit from a stray click or Enter, and forcing every
   blank before the learner may ask for judgement.
   Source: learner message; `v1/src/lib/core/answer-attempt.ts`.

Q2. Does the gate apply to all four answering surfaces?
A. Yes. The Answer button and the Enter-to-check key share one rule. Picks
   already required a choice; the rule now names that in the same place.
   Why: one gate cannot disagree with itself.
   Rules out: a keyboard path that bypasses the disabled button.
   Source: `v1/src/app/study/StudyCard.tsx`, `v1/src/lib/core/answer-attempt.ts`.

Q3. Who gets a completion, and on which cards?
A. Only a beginner deck, and only the one typed answer surface (type and
   hear-then-type). The level is the card's own deck level, resolved by
   `deckLevelFor`, the same level the teaching layer uses. No completion for
   cloze blanks or picks.
   Why: the learner said "auto complete fo beginner". Stronger levels should
   recall the line unaided; cloze blanks and picks answer in place and would
   need a different surface.
   Rules out: global-level completion, completion on every mode, a new card
   field.
   Source: learner message; `v1/src/app/StudyClient.tsx:125` (`deckLevelFor`).

Q4. When does the ghost appear, and what does it show?
A. After at least one typed character, when the typed text is a case- and
   diacritic-insensitive start of one of the card's expected answers. The rest
   of the expected answer shows as a muted suffix after the cursor. The typed
   part stays normal. The shortest remaining suffix wins when several expected
   answers match. No ghost before the first character, after a full match, or
   when nothing matches.
   Why: an empty field with the whole answer ghosted is a reveal, not an
   autocomplete; typing a start is the intent to answer. Folding case and
   diacritics matches the loose judge, so a keyboard without Polish letters
   still gets help.
   Rules out: fuzzy mid-word matching, a dropdown of answer choices, showing
   the answer before the learner starts.
   Source: `v1/src/lib/core/answer-completion.ts` (new), the judge's
   `normalizeAnswer` rule.

Q5. How is a completion accepted?
A. Tab, or the right arrow while the cursor is at the end of the line, or a
   small Complete control beside the input. Nothing is inserted on its own and
   Enter still checks the answer the learner typed. On a phone the Complete
   control is the route, because there is no Tab key.
   Why: autocomplete must never silently answer for the learner; a visible
   control keeps the choice explicit and touch-friendly.
   Rules out: accepting on Enter, accepting on any keystroke, a hidden gesture,
   auto-filling when the input blurs.
   Source: learner asked for autocomplete; the card's existing Enter-to-check
   rule stays.

Q6. Does using a completion count as help that caps the outcome?
A. No. A completion does not cap a Got it at Nearly. It is a teaching aid, like
   the teaching layer, not a hint. The hint cap stays exactly as it is.
   Why: the app already distinguishes teaching from hints ("Help is teaching,
   not a hint: the layer never caps the outcome"). The completion teaches the
   spelling and shape of the line; a hint answers the question.
   Rules out: silently turning a completed answer into Nearly, and a second
   evidence flag that the record does not need.
   Source: `v1/src/app/StudyClient.tsx:564` (the existing help rule),
   `docs/features/teaching-layers/PRD-TRD-teaching-layers.md`.

Q7. What happens to a learner who is stuck?
A. A quiet "I don't know" control submits an empty attempt on purpose, reveals
   the answer and records the card as missed. It sits apart from the Answer
   button, which stays disabled until the learner types. It is not offered on
   picks, where the options are the attempt.
   Why: a gate with no exit traps a learner who does not know the card. An
   explicit reveal keeps the record honest and the session moving.
   Rules out: a hidden skip, an automatic reveal after a timer, enabling the
   Answer button with empty input.
   Source: agent decision, delegated 2026-10-28.

Q8. What sound is added?
A. One short, soft cue when a completion is accepted, played only when the
   learner has sound on. The existing Got it, Nearly, Missed and Finish cues
   are unchanged, and sound stays off until the learner turns it on.
   Why: the learner asked for sound effects; the completion is the one new
   action that has no cue yet. A second outcome sound would compete with the
   judged cue that follows.
   Rules out: a key-click loop, sounds on every keystroke, autoplay.
   Source: learner message; `v1/src/lib/client/sound.ts`.

Q9. What does not change?
A. The judge, the evidence shape, the session loop, the plan, the provider and
   the privacy boundary do not change. No card is stored differently. The
   completion reads only the card's own expected answers and the learner's typing;
   nothing leaves the machine.
   Why: this is a card-level guide, not a new learning model.
   Rules out: a second answer field, a new outcome, a provider call for hints,
   storing the ghost or the acceptance.
   Source: agent decision under the learner's scope.
