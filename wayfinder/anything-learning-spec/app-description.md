# The app, described

*A description with the UI in view. This document is derived, not a decision: every claim below quotes the ticket that locked it, and where a later ticket amended an earlier one, the amendment is applied and the reversal is named. If anything here disagrees with a ticket, the ticket wins.*

## 1. What it is

From the map's destination:

> "…an anything-learning system: subject content arrives as plug-in packs over one flat card shape, A, I, generated cards are reviewed by the learner before they count, progress is recorded as evidence and read as strengths and weaknesses, and an algorithm the learner owns decides what is shown next. Everything runs on the learner's own machine; no learner data on any server; the A, I, provider is the learner's own key."

One clause of that destination has since been withdrawn: **"A, I, generated cards are reviewed by the learner before they count"** is gone — see [The A, I, tutor](tickets/ai-tutor-session.md), decision 12: "There is no proposal state, no per-card human judgement, and no accept step." The amendment is written into [The generation gate](tickets/generation-gate.md) as well, deliberately, "so neither ticket can be read without the other."

Out of scope, so no interface is owed for them: "Accounts and logins. Any server holding learner data. Sharing packs between people. A mobile app. A hosted or web-hosted version."

## 2. Opening it

From [How the learner opens the app](tickets/how-you-open-it.md):

> "A browser tab on the learner's own machine. The learner starts a local Express process and opens a URL served from loopback. Angular is the interface, served by Express, on the learner's machine, and nowhere else."

The build requirement that follows, and it is strict:

> "every provider call is made by the local process. The browser sends the prompt to loopback and nothing else. No key, no provider call, and no prompt construction happens in the front end. A build in which Angular holds the key is a build that has drifted from this decision."

Accepted costs of the browser: "a visible process the learner must start, and a browser tab rather than an application icon."

## 3. Before the first card: the plan

From [One session, end to end](tickets/session-loop.md):

> "**The plan is shown before the session starts.** … the learner sees the plan and the reason in plain words before beginning - for example, 'twenty cards, sixteen from past tense because you have been getting them wrong, four from vocabulary because you are strong there and they check you are still strong'."

> "The app never receives or stores a reason."

And the length:

> "**There is no fixed session length.** The app offers a number - twenty cards as a default offer - as an offer and not a rule. The learner may stop at any point."

So: the plan screen names the cards, the count, and the reason in plain words — including, when it applies, the ugly one the ticket accepts: "no idea, these came first".

## 4. The card on screen

### 4.1 Shape

From [What goes on a card](tickets/card-shape.md):

> "Cards are two-sided: a prompt side and an answer side. A one-sided card is a card with an empty side; no separate card type."

> "Each side is an ordered list of parts. The common part is text. Any part may be an attachment (audio, image, or anything else a pack defines). The core lays parts out in order and does not know what any part means."

What rides along, all visible as plain things:

- Tags: "A card carries plain tags for finer detail (grammar, difficulty, topic) that the pack understands but the core treats as opaque strings."
- Summary: "Every card carries a plain-text summary, built by the pack."
- Origin: "A card records its origin - written by the learner, generated by the provider, or imported - as a note only. Origin never changes card behaviour, so an edited generated card is simply the learner's."

### 4.2 Answering surfaces

From [How a card is answered — the answering surface](tickets/answering-surface.md). Four surfaces exist, and the pack hosts the choice:

> "a dotted blank exists because the question is asked inside a sentence; a chip list exists because the answer has a known set; one typed answer exists because there is nothing to point at."

Plus the fourth: hear-then-type, "including hear-then-type".

> "**Which surface a given card gets depends on the question, and that choice belongs to the pack/deck — never a global setting.** … The same deck can carry all four side by side, and the deck decides, card by card."

### 4.3 The answering rule

From [Evidence, and reading it as strength and weakness](tickets/evidence-model.md):

> "1. The card shows the question.
> 2. The learner answers before seeing the answer - by choosing from a fixed set where the card offers one, or by typing in their own words.
> 3. The answer is always shown."

### 4.4 The four outcomes

Also from the evidence model, verbatim:

- **Again** - "wrong, but familiar. A lapse in something already known."
- **Got it** - "correct."
- **Nearly** - "substantially correct, a word or detail slipped."
- **No** - "wrong, and it was not previously known. A gap."

> "Lapse and gap are kept apart in the record itself… because a lapse is a slip in existing knowledge and a gap is missing knowledge, and an algorithm must treat them differently."

With judging off, "the answer is recorded as **unjudged**, which is a distinct state from wrong, and the record can tell them apart" ([The generation gate](tickets/generation-gate.md), still standing after the amendment: "Judging stays configurable, per card and per pack.")

### 4.5 Who judges

From the evidence model:

> "The core judges nothing. It asks who decides. Judgement is pluggable and configurable by the learner, per pack and per card:
> - **Laya** - when the card offers a fixed set of answers. Local, free, roughly 33 ms. Laya picks from a supplied answer set and cannot hallucinate.
> - **The provider** - when the learner typed free text, where no answer set exists and equivalence must be judged. Costs money.
> - **The learner** - when they choose it, or when no judge is configured or reachable."

> "Nothing breaks when no judge is available. The answer still shows, the learner states the outcome, and the row is still written."

### 4.6 Hints, and what a hint costs

From [The A, I, tutor](tickets/ai-tutor-session.md):

> "**A hint costs the point: a helped card cannot be recorded as 'Got it'. The strongest cap available is 'Nearly'…**"

> "**A requested hint is capped exactly like an offered one.**"

> "**asking is never rationed and never withheld to save money.**"

Hints may be written or spoken, and the pack declares which: "A hint or correction may be written or spoken, and the pack declares which… The tutor supplies the words either way." The accessibility consequence is stated as the point: "the tutor writes the words and the voice merely reads them. The text always exists, because it is what the voice is reading."

### 4.7 Showing working

From the tutor ticket, decision 17:

> "**Two things are judged, not one: the answer reached, and how it was reached.**"

> "judging the method requires the learner to **show it**, so cards need a place to show working beside the place to give the answer. That is a second kind of editable region alongside the dotted blank, pack-declared like every other surface."

And decision 18:

> "**The learner's shown working is shown back to them, beside the correct one.** Not folded silently into a score, not discarded: the learner sees their own route next to the working route, so they can see *where* it went wrong."

Then it is gone: "the learner's shown working is displayed during the answer and then **discarded - never stored**."

### 4.8 The tutor during the study screen

From the tutor ticket:

> "**The tutor teaches mid-session, not only picks and generates.** The tutor may interrupt the card flow: a hint before the learner answers, a correction after, a line of encouragement. … It speaks on its own initiative rather than only when asked."

> "It may interrupt on its own judgement the way a human tutor does - before, during, or after a card - and the learner's response is the only brake."

> "**The tutor talks in texts, not in an open line.** Every exchange is a message out and a message back. There is no held-open connection of any kind…"

> "**The tutor reacts to what the learner answered, never to time.**"

> "**The tutor cannot nag.** … Silence belongs to the learner. This is a flashcard, not a proctor."

When the provider dies mid-session: "the tutor degrades to local rather than vanishing… the tutor's teaching stops while the session continues. Silence is not a bug; it is the absence of a provider."

And the cost, accepted and visible rather than capped: "**No ceiling on what the tutor may spend.** Every call is a call, the price is named, and the learner pays it."

### 4.9 Wrong cards return

From the session loop:

> "**A card answered wrongly returns at the end of the same session, once.** Not in three days, not at the algorithm's spaced position."

## 5. The ending of a session

From the session loop:

> "**Stopping is not an ending.** No summary screen, no report, no sign-off, nothing evaluative. The learner closes the app and the next session plans afresh from the evidence that exists, including the fact of stopping."

And throughout, the no-nag rule:

> "It does not nag, does not display a streak, does not show guilt, does not withhold weak cards as leverage."

## 6. Progress

From [Reading progress back](tickets/progress-view.md):

> "**No scores anywhere.** No percentage, no grade, no level bar, no streak. The screen shows named things, and each named thing carries a link to the raw evidence beneath it."

> "**The screen answers one question: what can I do now that I could not do before.** It shows cards, not metrics…"

> "**Familiarity is never sold as progress.** A card counts as improved only if the learner's early attempts were genuinely poor."

> "**Weakness appears as work to do, never as a lack.** The screen says 'past tense is next', never 'you are bad at past tense'."

> "**No comparison to other people.** No percentile, no average learner, no leaderboard, no rank."

Inspection and correction:

> "The learner inspects anything and corrects evidence, but never edits a conclusion directly. Any displayed conclusion can be opened down to the evidence behind it, and the learner can add a correction row when evidence is wrong. Conclusions themselves are computed on read and are not editable."

Progress belongs to cards, not packs:

> "Uninstalling a pack does not remove the learner's cards or their evidence, and reinstalling the pack does not restore or reset anything. Deleting cards deletes their evidence, and that is a deliberate learner act."

## 7. Privacy moments in the interface

From [What the learner knows, and what leaves their machine](tickets/privacy-boundary.md):

> "The learner sees the exact prompt before it is sent, with a copy action beside the send, and the app keeps a local log of what was sent and when."

> "A prompt contains only app-chosen categories about the learner - weak topics, level, learning style - and never anything the learner wrote."

Pack requests for learner text are per item, never blanket:

> "Inclusion is per item, deliberate, and visible: the learner selects the item, sees exactly what will be sent, and confirms that one item. Install-time blanket consent is forbidden…"

And the tutor is bounded the same way, through a standing banner:

> "Before the session begins the learner sees the full shape of every prompt the tutor will send while that session runs - the standing request, the weak topics, the level, the learning style, the card text already decided to be shown, and the shape of the tutor's own instructions - and approves it once."

> "The banner is per session and does not persist; each new session asks again."

The key, and its failures, said plainly:

> "The provider key is stored in the operating system key store (Credential Manager / Keychain / Secret Service), not a plain file."

> "A failed, expired, missing, or unreachable provider is reported in plain words, never retried silently forever, and never blocks work in progress. A missing key is never a startup blocker."

And from the tutor ticket, what also leaves: "Time taken is recorded locally and sent later, in its own batch, after the learner has answered. It is never part of the live reply…"

## 8. Generated cards in the interface

The current state, after the amendment recorded in [The generation gate](tickets/generation-gate.md):

> "**Generated decks are machine-validated and accepted, not reviewed by hand.** … The system checks that the output is well-formed and that blanks are placed in sane positions, and then the cards are cards. There is no proposal state, no per-card judgement, and no accept button."

What survives the reversal:

> "**Editing still makes a brand new card**, zero history. Untouched."
> "**The origin note still names the generation request** on every generated card, so a generated card is never mistaken for a pack's own material."
> "**The three app-owned prompt slots stand** as the shape of a generation prompt."
> "**Judging stays configurable**, per card and per pack. Untouched."

(And the tutor's prompt is recorded as "a **fourth app-owned shape**", "owed rather than silently absorbed" into that ticket.)

The deepest move, from the tutor ticket, decision 13:

> "**The model writes the whole correct thing; the app makes the exercise out of it.** … The model is never asked to place a blank correctly, because the model never places blanks. It writes truth; the app derives the exercise."

> "The full line is always available. The learner can see the complete thing the card was made from…"

What replaces the review screen as the safety net:

> "A card that is false cannot hide, because every time it is shown it produces bad evidence, and a card missed repeatedly while its neighbours are fine looks exactly like a card that is wrong. … the full line is always present on the card, so a false card is wrong in a way the learner can see rather than in a way they must trust."

A tutor's teaching becomes a card like any other: "the teaching is not chatter that evaporates and not a note bolted onto an existing card. It becomes a **card**…" — and after the amendment, that card is machine-validated, not reviewed.

## 9. Packs and imports in the interface

From [A pack's shape, and the library-ready gate](tickets/pack-shape.md):

> "the core looks in one known folder on the learner's machine at startup, treating it like a library into which albums are dropped. There is no server and nothing to browse."

> "A broken pack never touches learner data and never prevents the app from opening. The app names the broken pack, states plainly what is wrong with it, and continues with everything else."

From the card ticket, what import looks like when it goes wrong and when it goes right:

> "Importing cards is all-or-nothing. A malformed card rejects the whole batch with an error naming what is wrong and where."

> "Cards are imported in chunks, not one giant paste."

> "An out-of-place card (well-formed but belongs elsewhere) is a judgement, not a rule, so it is asked of a pluggable checker and the card arrives flagged for the learner to accept or reject - never blocked. If no checker is available or the provider is unreachable, the batch still lands unflagged."

## 10. Quiet receipts (settings)

From the session loop, what fallback looks like afterwards:

> "Failures are counted, and a quiet line in settings names which algorithm ran. Not an error, not a warning - just the name, so a learner who looks one day can notice the fallback has been running since March."

Also from the session loop: the app sanity-checks what its algorithm returned — "enough cards, enough unseen cards, enough variety" — and "Implausible output counts as failure and falls back, without requiring a crash." In the moment it is invisible; on reflection it is the settings line above.

## 11. What the interface must never do

Each line here is a decision, not styling:

- Never show a score, percentage, grade, level bar, or streak ([Reading progress back](tickets/progress-view.md)).
- Never show a summary screen, a report, or a sign-off when the learner stops ([One session, end to end](tickets/session-loop.md)).
- Never nag, show guilt, or withhold weak cards as leverage ([One session, end to end](tickets/session-loop.md)).
- Never compare the learner to other people ([Reading progress back](tickets/progress-view.md)).
- Never let the page hold the key, make a provider call, or construct a prompt ([How the learner opens the app](tickets/how-you-open-it.md)).
- Never send learner-written text in a prompt without the learner selecting that one item and confirming it ([What the learner knows…](tickets/privacy-boundary.md)).
- Never block work in progress when a provider fails, and never block startup on a missing key ([What the learner knows…](tickets/privacy-boundary.md)).
- Never block the app on a broken pack, and never half-apply one ([A pack's shape…](tickets/pack-shape.md)).
- Never block an out-of-place card — flag it for the learner to accept or reject ([What goes on a card](tickets/card-shape.md)).
- Never ask the learner to review generated cards one by one — there is no review screen ([The A, I, tutor](tickets/ai-tutor-session.md), decision 12).

## 12. Not yet decided — so the interface must not pretend

From the map's "Not yet specified", the items that touch the interface:

- **Should generation be batch-aware** — an open ticket, "largely dissolved, not yet closed": "The tutor ticket withdrew the per-card review that made batches a UX problem; forty generated cards are now simply forty cards. Revisit and close rather than grill."
- **A deck you brought yourself: import without A, I** — an open ticket. "the whole app must be worth using with every generation feature switched off"; import as JSON file or pasted JSON; what the origin note says; whether the checker runs in warning mode — all undecided.
- **Multi-blank cards produce several answers on one card** — "Whether a row can carry a region index, or a blank is its own micro-card, is an addendum to T3 that a future resolution answers." The walkthrough's per-blank judgment is "an interim".
- **Grouping is a list, and T4's wording owes the correction** — "A card belongs to a *list* of topics, not one." Route via drift or surgical-change back into T4's resolution.
- **The judge seam / free recall** — "many answers can be right, which is the normal case for a code card"; local fine-tuning or pack-authored equivalence rules, "Neither chosen." Free recall "costs money by default".
- **Categories can still be identifying** — whether prompt categories are "generalised, coarsened, or bounded is undecided."
- **What the checker defaults to when it abstains** — "Neither is decided."
- **Attachment storage** — "where the bytes live, how they are referenced, and what the pack supplies versus what the learner supplies is undecided."
- **Deck lifecycle** — "how a learner creates, renames, merges, and deletes decks" undecided.
- **Retention and forgetting** — "when a card leaves the deck, and whether evidence decays… no ticket decides how long a gap stays a gap."
- **Error and absence handling** — "what the app does when a provider is unreachable, a prompt is refused, or a pack is malformed" — partially covered by the privacy ticket's plain-words rules, not fully decided.
- **Onboarding polish, notifications, streaks** — "named but not designed" (map destination).

## 13. What held, on the hardest subject

From [Polish, end to end](tickets/polish-walkthrough.md), the evidence that the decisions express a real subject without the core learning what the subject is:

> "Every locked decision expressed Polish without the core learning what Polish is. The plain-text summary, two-sided card shape, append-only evidence, four outcomes, no-scores progress, pack-as-data, and three prompt slots all carried a language the core knows nothing about."

The walkthrough artifact itself, still the best single view of the intended experience: [`wireframe/polish-walkthrough/experience/index.html`](../../wireframe/polish-walkthrough/experience/index.html) — "the full interactive experience: plan, then cards you type into, chips, and listen-and-type; reveal; four outcomes; wrong cards return; the no-report ending."
